## Hi there 👋

I build the things that should already exist.

Most of what I make starts as a question: why does this work the way it does, and why not some better way. When the answer comes back as "that is just how it is," I treat that as the starting line instead of the finish, and go build the version that proves the answer wrong.

The gaps I like best are the ones nobody bothered with. Not because they were hard (most of the time), but because they were unglamorous enough that everyone walked past them. Somebody has to notice the missing piece and actually make it, and that tends to be me. A good share of what I build is a bridge, too: two tools that were always meant to talk to each other, finally doing it, because I got tired of waiting for someone official to wire them together.

I do not stick to one lane. Games, developer tooling, media servers, AI orchestration, whatever the problem in front of me happens to need. The throughline under all of it is the person using the thing. I want them walking away with more range than they came in with, more of what the machine can actually do and less of what someone else decided they should be allowed to. Some of these are built to earn their keep, and I am fine with that, but capability is the point. If a tool leaves you more capable than it found you, it did its job.

Things I have made and released:
Plexxarr collapses the whole Plex automation stack into one app. It is the bridge between "I want to watch this" and it simply being there, correctly named and filed, with no wall of Docker services in the way.
Claw Deck is a command deck for the models running on your own machine, and unlike most of its neighbors it actually checks what it installs. Everyone was running plugins and servers on blind faith, so I built the part that vets them first.
HumbleRedeemer deals with the pile of Humble keys quietly rotting in your account. It claims the games you forgot you owned, redeems what you are missing, and rounds up your duplicates so you can hand them to someone who will use them.
Job Finder rebuilds the job hunt around the person applying instead of the company hiring. It grades listings against your real experience, tailors your CV/resume from your own history, and keeps every last byte of it on your machine.
